ACM UIST 2026 Workshop

Proxemics of Physical AI

Opportunities and Challenges of Augmenting Intimate, Personal, Social, and Public Spaces

When
Monday, November 2, 2026
1:45 to 5:00 PM (EST)
Where
Marquette B, Detroit Marriott at the Renaissance Center, Detroit, MI
Registration
Through UIST 2026 registration. Seats are limited.

Physical AI, systems that perceive, reason, and act through actuated bodies, is moving interaction off the screen and into the space around us. Wearable robots and on-skin interfaces now operate inside the wearer's intimate space, while robotics foundation models and simulation platforms bring embodied agents into shared social and public settings. Our understanding of how these systems should occupy human spaces, and how they should interact with people within them, has not kept pace.

Edward Hall's proxemics describes how people regulate the space around them across four zones: intimate (under 0.45 m), personal (0.45 to 1.2 m), social (1.2 to 3.6 m), and public (beyond 3.6 m). This half-day workshop asks what proxemics means when the machines are worn, shared, autonomous, and everywhere. Through a keynote, hands-on provocations in each zone, and group discussion, we will work towards a framework for the Proxemics of Physical AI and a shared research roadmap.

Scope of the Workshop

We will discuss four themes grounded in Hall's proxemics.

01

Design Dimensions and Collaboration Strategies

Design vocabularies at the level of zones and body segments; coordination across multiple limbs, bodies, or agents; and how tasks, initiative, and collaboration are shared between people and Physical AI.

02

Agency and Control

Agency and ownership under shared and blended control; autonomy gradients and arbitration; legible, predictable motion as a substitute for distance; intent cueing; and behavioral, subjective, and physiological measures of agency across Hall's zones.

03

Adaptation Strategies and Challenges

Humans adapt to machines, and machines adapt to humans. We will reflect on mutual and long-term adaptation, personalization across users and cultures, strategies for learning proxemic behavior, sim-to-real transfer, and lessons from failed deployments.

04

Ethics, Privacy, Safety, and Social Acceptance

Hall's zones are cultural, not universal. We will consider perceived versus functional safety, bystander expectations, the acceptability of augmented bodies across cultures, generations, and abilities, and models of accountability and responsibility.

Keynote

Masahiko Inami

Professor, Research Center for Advanced Science and Technology (RCAST), The University of Tokyo

We are delighted to welcome Masahiko Inami as our keynote speaker. His research in Information Somatics explores human-computer integration, from enhancing human input and output to designing new body schemas, including the JIZAI Body work on wearable robotic arms shown in our teaser. His projects include the Transparent Cockpit and Superhuman Sports. He received the Kenjiro Takayanagi Achievement Award and the Commendation for Science and Technology by Japan's Minister of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology.
